**3.	Match Dates**

Write a program, which matches a date in the format **"dd{separator}MMM{separator}yyyy"**.

**Compose the Regular Expression** </br>
Every valid date has the following characteristics: </br>
&emsp; •	Always starts with **two digits**, followed by a **separator** </br>
&emsp; •	After that, it has **one uppercase** and two **lowercase letters** (e.g. **Jan, Mar**). </br>
&emsp; •	After that, it has a **separator** and **exactly 4 digits** (for the year). </br>
&emsp; •	The separator could be either of three things: a period **(".")**, a hyphen **("-")** or a forward-slash **("/")** </br>
&emsp; •	The separator needs to be **the same** for the whole date (e.g. 13.03.2016 is valid, 13.03/2016 is **NOT**). Use a **group backreference** to check for this.
